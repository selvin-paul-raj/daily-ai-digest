## Post as published

Apple is tightening macOS Full Disk Access, and it named AI agents as the reason.

In a developer note on Oct 2, Apple said Full Disk Access "largely sidesteps" macOS privacy controls and that some apps use it to expose files, mail, messages and browsing history without users fully understanding it. Granting it will now take "very explicit user action." The trigger: an Inc. columnist's claim that Meta's Muse agent read his private messages, which Meta disputes.

My take: desktop agents have been riding on a permission built for backup apps. If your agent needs the whole disk to work, that is a design smell. Ask for the narrowest scope that does the job, and assume every OS will make broad access harder from here.

On the infra side, Supabase raised $150M, is buying Turso (SQLite-based databases for agents) and launched Supabase Compute, hosted sandboxes for long-running agents.

Also worth your time:
1. MIT and Sakana's SIFT reached 35.1% on Polyglot in under 5 hours for about $150 by letting an LLM judge rank candidates before full evals.
2. Nvidia's 64GB DGX Spark systems start at $4,999 on Oct 23.

Would you give a coding agent Full Disk Access on your work laptop?
