## Post as published

An AI found the bug on Wednesday. Attackers were exploiting it on Thursday.

Horizon3 used Anthropic's Mythos to find CVE-2026-61500 in Rejetto HFS. The model worked out that V8's Math.random() leaked enough output to recover the xorshift128+ state with the Z3 solver, then forge signed session cookies for admin access and RCE. VulnCheck saw exploitation the next day. Of 286 CVEs tied to Mythos and Project Glasswing, it is only the second known to be exploited. Fix: HFS v3.2.1.

The other side of the same week: Google stopped taking product bug reports to its open source bounty on 1 Oct, after a flood of invalid AI-generated submissions. Update promised by Q1 2027.

My take: AI has made finding bugs cheap. Triage and patching have not kept up, and that gap is now measured in hours, not months.

Also worth your time:
- GitLab fixed CVE-2026-90970 (CVSS 9.9): a Duo Agent Platform user could escape the prompt-template sandbox on self-hosted AI Gateways. Patch to 19.2.4, 19.3.2 or 19.4.1.
- Strata runs the 125B Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on a 12 GB GPU, 94 tokens/s on an RTX 5070 at Q2_0. MIT licensed.

If you run agents, how fast can you ship a security patch today?
