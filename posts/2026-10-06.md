## Post as published

Reflection just showed a 501B open-weight model, and the key number is not a benchmark score.

Beam is a sparse MoE with 23B active parameters, pretrained on 23.8T tokens. Then the RL: over 100 million rollouts on 10.5K GB300 GPUs for four weeks, and Reflection says scores were still climbing. Its own numbers: 80.1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 77.2 on SWE-Bench Pro v2-Hard, and GLM-5.2 level reasoning at 3 to 4x less inference compute. Weights land later this month.

Same day, an MIT paper: fixing a base model's opening cue to "Okay" took Olmo-3-7B from 42% to 78% on MATH-500, close to its RL-trained version. Their reading: RL mostly makes those cues more likely.

My take: both can be true. RL may mostly surface what the base model already knows, and doing that reliably across a million environments is still the hard part. Before you fine-tune, test a two-token prefill on your own eval.

Also worth your time:
- A 7-agent OpenAI Dots swarm raised a 1979 covering bound, C(24,14,4), from 19 to 20, with a Lean proof. Not peer reviewed yet.
- Looped Models Part II: a 1.6B looped LM matches full-cache quality with a 3x smaller KV cache.

What moves your agents more: a better base model or a better RL environment?

## Stories tracked

1. Reflection AI Beam (5 Oct): 501B MoE, 23B active, 23.8T pretraining tokens; RL with 100M+ rollouts on 10.5K GB300 GPUs over 4 weeks, ~1.3B sandboxes, 1M environments; self-reported Terminal-Bench 2.1 80.1, SWE-Bench Pro v2-Hard 77.2, MCP Atlas 78.7, GPQA 90.5; GLM-5.2-level reasoning at 3-4x less inference compute; weights later this month.
