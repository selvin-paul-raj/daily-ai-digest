## Post as published

Your vector index can redraw your documents. A new paper re-identified 98.4% of pages from stored vectors alone.

The setup: ColPali-style visual retrievers store each page as about 1,000 patch vectors, often in a vector database run by a third party. Since nobody can read a vector, the index gets treated as less sensitive than the page.

The paper tested that. A flow-matching inverter redrew pages from raw Tomoro-ColQwen3-8B indices on ViDoRe v3 (19,252 pages). It recovered 47% of words and 45% of sensitive tokens, and the redrawn pages ranked their source page first 98.4% of the time. Shuffling the vectors did not help: a position model restored the order and pushed re-identification from 3.8% to 93.5%. The same attack, unchanged, still hit 70.2% on a second retriever. Token pooling was the one defence it did not break.

My take: if your RAG stack sends page embeddings to a managed vector DB, that DB holds a lossy copy of your documents. Give it the same access controls, encryption and vendor review as the source files, and test pooling before you need it.

Does your team classify embeddings as sensitive data today?
