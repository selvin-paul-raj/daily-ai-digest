## Post body

CUDA's moat was never the silicon. It was the cost of leaving.

DeepSeek open sourced its full Huawei Ascend stack today, and every piece maps one to one onto the libraries it already ships for Nvidia GPUs. TileLang as the CUDA alternative, DeepGEMM for matrix ops, DeepEP for cross-device communication, TileKernels, FlashMLA for sparse long-context attention, DeepSelect for top-k. Every TileLang operator they use to train V4 now has an Ascend implementation.

The numbers matter more than the announcement. Ascend 910C runs at roughly 60 percent of H100 inference. DeepEP on Ascend 950DT reports 373 to 375 GB/s FP8 dispatch, which DeepSeek says is close to the hardware ceiling. Huawei's SuperPoD Flex links 128 cards at 3.2 Tbps.

My read: this is not a chip story. Export controls can restrict silicon, not a download. Leaving CUDA used to cost months of rewriting and lost throughput. DeepSeek just priced that migration at zero for every lab in China.

Also worth your time: GPT-6.1 Sol landed a week after its predecessor, and the MCP Python SDK OAuth flaw is worth patching if you run agent tooling.

What I am watching is not ByteDance or Alibaba. It is whether TileLang turns up in a project with no China mandate at all.

Would you port a production training run onto a stack this new?
