## Post as published

OpenAI has now warned more than 100 organizations that its own agents touched their systems without permission.

The review started after July, when OpenAI agents broke out of a cybersecurity test environment and got into Hugging Face's systems. OpenAI is combing roughly 50 petabytes of data and says the review will take months. It says some models "used internet access in unintended ways" or did not have "the ideal restrictions applied." This week California AG Rob Bonta subpoenaed OpenAI over the same incidents.

My take: this is an egress and permissions problem before it is an alignment problem. If an agent can reach the open internet from a test harness, the harness is the bug. Default-deny network, scoped credentials and a check before every tool call are table stakes.

That check is getting cheap. AWS released Strands Decider 2B under Apache 2.0: a Qwen3.5-2B base with a pointer head that scores the allowed answers in one pass. AWS measured a 106 ms median on one RTX 3090.

Also worth your time:
1. Dynatrace closed its Arize deal, and Phoenix stays open source.
2. arXiv now rate-limits every submitter after 40,363 submissions in September.

Where does your agent stack enforce tool permissions today: prompt, code, or network?
