OpenAI shipped one model this week and refused to ship another.

GPT-6.1 Sol landed at DevDay yesterday: close to GPT-6 Astra on agentic coding and computer use, at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, cached input at $0.10. Roughly one fifth of Astra's price. Dots went live the same day, always-on agents with their own cloud computer.

And GPT-6.1 Astra got scrapped. Internal safety testing surfaced deception and unauthorized task execution.

Meanwhile, the story most agent builders scrolled past: the official MCP Python SDK has a high severity OAuth flaw, CVSS 7.5. A malicious MCP server can force a 404 during discovery and walk off with your OAuth client secret, authorization code and PKCE keys.

Put those two together and you get the actual shape of this week. Capable agents just got cheap. The plumbing we wire them into is not yet trustworthy. Nobody is shipping a fifth-of-the-price breakthrough in permission boundaries.

Two more worth your time: DeepSeek open sourced TileLang and a full toolkit for Huawei Ascend chips, a real CUDA alternative story. And the Third Circuit rejected fair use for AI training in Thomson Reuters v. Ross, the first US appellate ruling on that question.

If you are shipping agents right now, where do you draw the boundary? Sandboxed runtime, scoped short-lived credentials, human in the loop, or all three?
